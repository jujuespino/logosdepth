Memorize scripture and keep it

LogosDepth helps you learn a verse word for word, then brings it back at the right times so it stays.

Start with a verse

How it works

  1. Learn in short steps. Read the verse, practice it phrase by phrase, fill in the blanks, put the pieces in order, then type it from memory.
  2. Come back on a schedule. After you learn a verse, it returns after 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60 and 120 days. A clean recall moves it up a step; a miss brings it back tomorrow.
  3. Mastered means recalled on three different days. One good session is not mastery, so the app asks for it on separate days.

You are not limited to the starter packs: the whole Bible is there (all 66 books, King James Version, and the World English Bible where it lines up). Pick any verse or short passage and learn it the same way.

It works offline, can be added to your home screen, and keeps your progress in your own browser. You can back it up, and (if this site offers it) sync across devices with an emailed sign-in link.

The science behind it

The design follows research on how people learn and keep verbal material. We have tried to say only what the evidence supports, including where it is thin.

What the app doesWhyStrength of evidence
You type the verse from memory instead of re-reading itTrying to recall strengthens memory more than studying again, and the advantage grows with time. Free recall tends to help more than picking from choices.Strong, replicated
Reviews are spread over days and weeksSpreading practice out improves long-term retention compared with cramming.Strong, replicated
A verse is mastered after recall on three separate daysRelearning across sessions, not one good session, is what builds durable memory.Strong
Verses are split into phrases to practiceTesting yourself during learning, not only at the end, helps; working in small chunks keeps practice winnable.Moderate
Optional “guess first” before readingAttempting before you have learned something, even wrongly, improves later learning of that material.Moderate
Rewards (XP, streaks) follow successful recall, not time spentGame elements help on average but can backfire if they reward the wrong thing.Mixed

What we do not know

  • We did not find a study that tested Bible-verse memorization itself. The research comes from word lists, texts and school subjects, and we are applying it to verses.
  • The research supports spacing practice out, but not any one exact schedule. The 1, 3, 7, 14, 30, 60, 120 day ladder is our design choice.
  • Apps cannot make memorizing effortless. The methods work because they ask effort of you at the right moments.
